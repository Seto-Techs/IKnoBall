## 5. How picks and points work

1. **One pick per game, two boards.** Pick the winner. One submission records it on both:
   - **Flat** — 1 point if you're right, 0 if not.
   - **Weighted** — if you're right, points equal the fair price locked when you submitted, times
     10 and rounded, capped at 150. If you're wrong, 0.
2. **Picks close at tip-off.** You can place, change, or remove a pick until the game starts. The
   cutoff is the scheduled tip-off stored on our servers, not the clock on your device. A delayed
   game that hasn't started may stay open; a game that has started is closed.
3. **Your price locks when you submit** and settlement uses only that locked price — never a later
   one.
4. **Sometimes there's no price.** Your weighted pick is still recorded and marked as awaiting a
   line. If a price shows up later we use the opening line. If a game finishes and no price ever
   appeared, that weighted pick is **voided** rather than scored zero — it counts as neither a win
   nor a loss.
5. **Settlement** happens automatically after a game finishes, from the official score. It runs on
   a schedule, so points can take a little while to appear.
6. **Postponed or cancelled games** don't reach a final result, so picks on them are voided and
   drop off the leaderboard.
7. **Corrections.** If a score, result, or price turns out to be wrong, we can fix the affected
   picks and standings. We'll do it as soon as we reasonably can.
8. **Voiding picks.** We can void a pick or remove points if we reasonably believe it came from a
   bug, an exploit, a data error, or a breach of these terms — including bots, duplicate accounts,
   or fiddling with the odds feed.
9. **Leaderboards** are built from settled picks only. Ties are broken however we reasonably
   choose. A leaderboard position isn't a prize or an entitlement.
10. Our call on whether a pick counts, what price was locked, and what a game's result was is
    final, as long as we act reasonably and in good faith.
